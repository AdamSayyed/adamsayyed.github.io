Background

I am a senior Computer Science student with hands-on experience building AI and software systems through internships and research at Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, Sandia National Laboratories, and UMBC.

At Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, I work as a Software Engineering Intern developing data-intensive systems for sensor analysis. I built an internal FastAPI service used by 20 analysts to convert simulated sensor data into standardized tactical formats, implementing hundreds of schema validations and data transformations across hundreds of datasets and reducing manual data preparation by 65%. I also developed scalable Python pipelines for passive radar processing, improving processing speed by 60% while reducing data-quality failures by 70%.

I developed ML models in Python and C++, built automated error-analysis pipelines for object detection, and integrated Large Language Models through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to expose automated functions for geospatial analytics.

At UMBC, my research focuses on multimodal machine learning for financial forecasting. I built an end-to-end pipeline combining financial time-series data with transformer-based sentiment embeddings from fine-tuned FinBERT and RoBERTa models, integrating data from multiple live sources and developing the preprocessing and temporal-alignment infrastructure required to train the models.

My earlier research at the University of Iowa involved implementing and optimizing sequence-to-sequence Transformers for German-English machine translation. I also gained software development experience building interactive applications and data-driven systems, giving me a foundation that spans backend development, APIs, machine learning, distributed computing, and high-performance systems.

I am particularly interested in AI engineering, ML systems, AI infrastructure, high-performance computing, and the software engineering required to deploy intelligent systems at scale.
